What is this degree?

This is a business leadership degree for the AI era. It prepares students to lead, manage, and implement artificial intelligence within organizations, focusing on how people and AI systems work together to improve business performance, decision-making, and innovation.

Unlike traditional AI programs centered on model development, this degree is about applying AI in real organizations to run them better.

What jobs does this lead to?

Graduates are prepared for high-demand business roles increasingly requiring AI fluency, including:

  • Management Analyst
  • Project Management Specialist
  • Operations Manager
  • Business intelligence or strategy roles
  • AI implementation and change management roles

Labor market data show strong demand, with tens of thousands of annual openings statewide and employer preference for bachelor’s-level candidates.

Is this a technical degree?

This is an applied, business-focused degree designed around AI-enabled workforce roles. Students learn to use AI tools, interpret and apply data, design AI-supported workflows, evaluate ethical and business risks, and lead teams through AI adoption.

The program aligns directly with workforce demand for AI-capable business professionals who can bridge business and technology.

Why not just study Computer Science or AI engineering?

Most jobs using AI are not engineering jobs. AI skill demand is rapidly growing in marketing, operations, finance, management, and professional services. These roles require people who can translate AI into business value.

This degree fills the gap between AI system builders and traditional business graduates by preparing AI-enabled business leaders.

Why is a bachelor’s degree necessary for this field?

Employer data clearly show that 88% of relevant job postings with education requirements request a bachelor’s degree. These roles often involve decision-making, strategy, cross-functional leadership, business analysis, and implementation responsibility.

Employers increasingly expect both business knowledge and AI capability. This is the kind of above middle-skill workforce gap the CCCCO BDP framework is designed to address.

How is this different from an AI degree at a university?

Many AI degrees focus on programming, machine learning, and model development and are housed in Computer Science or Engineering. This program focuses on business application, leadership, implementation, human-AI collaboration, ethics, and organizational impact.

This is a management plus AI degree: applied AI for business leadership.

Why is this important right now?

AI adoption is accelerating, but organizations are still learning how to integrate it into operations, decision-making, and strategy. The biggest barrier is often talent: people who understand AI tools, can apply them to real business problems, and can lead change across organizations.

This degree directly addresses that gap by preparing graduates for AI-enabled decision-making and implementation in real organizations.

Who should enroll in this program?

This degree is ideal for business majors who want future-ready skills, working professionals looking to upskill into AI-enabled roles, students interested in leadership, strategy, or entrepreneurship, and individuals who want to work with AI in organizations.

Why is Fullerton College uniquely positioned to offer this?

Fullerton College is already a leader in AI applied to business education and is one of the only colleges offering an AI Business associate degree. The proposed bachelor’s degree builds directly on that foundation and creates a complete associate-to-baccalaureate pathway, a key expectation in the BDP framework.

The college is also located in a region with strong AI investment, business activity, and workforce demand.

How does this align with workforce and state priorities?

The program supports regional workforce needs for AI-enabled business talent, state goals for economic growth and workforce development, and employer demand for bachelor’s-level professionals in management, analytics, operations, and implementation roles.

The BDP application requires evidence of workforce demand, employer preference for bachelor’s degrees, higher wage outcomes, and student interest. This program is designed around those criteria.

How does the degree advance CCCCO Vision 2030?

The degree connects Vision 2030's goals for equity in access, success, and support with all three strategic directions: equitable baccalaureate attainment, equitable workforce and economic development, and generative AI and the future of learning.

More specifically, it can contribute to Vision 2030 Outcomes 1, 2, 3b, 4, and 5 covering access, completion, community college baccalaureate attainment, living-wage employment, and financial-aid support. It also directly aligns with Actions 6, 7, and 12 concerning baccalaureate expansion, workforce mobility, and GenAI literacy.

View the complete Vision 2030 crosswalk.

What evidence demonstrates a regional workforce need for this degree?

The May 2026 Orange County Center of Excellence assessment identified 15,835 annual openings across Los Angeles and Orange counties in the three target occupations: General and Operations Managers, Project Management Specialists, and Management Analysts.

The analysis also found 4,770 aligned California job postings. Among postings that listed an education requirement, 88% requested a bachelor's degree. Entry-level wages for the target occupations exceeded the single-adult living-wage benchmarks in both Orange and Los Angeles counties.

That quantitative evidence is reinforced by employer input, workforce-board support, and the Orange County Regional Consortium's recommendation. Review the labor market findings.

What makes this degree different from anything else?

Most programs teach students how to build AI. This program teaches students how to lead with AI. That distinction is where the workforce gap exists.

What is the current application status?

Fullerton College submitted the complete Cycle 9 application in August 2026. On October 2, 2026, the California Community Colleges Chancellor's Office granted the degree provisional approval after it passed the program compliance and quality review.

The proposal is now in intersegmental consultation, where CSU, UC, and AICCU review it for duplication (October 2 – November 30). Full approval also requires ACCJC substantive change approval and Board of Governors approval. See the full timeline.

Can the application link to Google Docs or external files?

The application instructions warn against embedded links to external documents. Supporting evidence should be attached separately, generally as PDFs or the specified Word/PDF narrative files.

What regional consultation was completed?

The submitted application includes employer and education-partner input, support letters, and joint support from the Anaheim, Orange County, and Santa Ana workforce boards. The Orange County Regional Consortium recommended the program on June 16, 2026.

Employer feedback and advisory participation remain welcome during review and implementation planning. Visit the Partners page.

What student-interest evidence was submitted?

The application includes a May-July 2026 survey of 25 current students and alumni. Among respondents, 76% were very or somewhat interested in the proposed degree, 56% said they would definitely or likely enroll, and 80% rated AI skills as important or extremely important to their careers.

The application projects an initial cohort of 25-35 students and 20-30 annual completers at full implementation. Review the student-interest findings.
