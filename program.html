Program Design

Program Overview

The proposed 120-unit Bachelor of Science follows a 2+2 pathway from associate-level AI in Business preparation into upper-division study in applied AI for business leadership, organizational strategy, ethics, and human-AI collaboration.

Draft Program Description

Business leadership for applying AI in real organizations.

The Bachelor of Science in Applied Human-AI Collaboration and Leadership prepares students to lead, design, and manage the integration of artificial intelligence within organizations. Unlike traditional AI programs focused primarily on programming and technical development, this program emphasizes the human, organizational, and strategic dimensions of AI adoption.

Students learn how to work alongside AI systems to enhance decision-making, improve productivity, and drive innovation across industries. The program integrates applied AI literacy, data-informed decision-making, organizational leadership, and ethical governance, equipping graduates to bridge business and technology.

Learning Outcomes

Students will be able to:

Apply AI Concepts and Tools

Analyze and apply artificial intelligence concepts and tools to improve organizational decision-making, operational efficiency, and innovation.

Interpret AI-Generated Insights

Use data analytics and AI-generated insights to support strategic and operational decisions.

Lead AI-Enabled Change

Lead cross-functional teams and manage organizational change associated with AI adoption and integration.

Govern Responsible AI

Evaluate ethical, legal, and societal implications and implement responsible, equitable, and compliant AI practices.

Develop AI Strategy

Create AI adoption strategies aligned with business objectives, workforce needs, and industry trends.

Communicate Across Audiences

Communicate complex AI concepts and solutions effectively to technical and non-technical stakeholders.

Design Applied Solutions

Design, develop, and present a real-world AI-enabled solution that addresses a defined organizational challenge.

Bridge People and Technology

Connect technical teams, business stakeholders, users, and organizational leaders around practical AI implementation.

Lower Division

AI in Business Foundation

The lower-division component includes Cal-GETC and major preparation through the Artificial Intelligence in Business AS or equivalent preparation.

  • BUS 108 F - Living in an Online World
  • BUS 256 F - Artificial Intelligence and Prompt Engineering for Business
  • BUS 257 F - AI Applications for Business
  • CIS 201 F - Introduction to Python Programming
  • CIS 258 F - Applied AI: Machine Learning, Deep Learning and NLP

Upper Division

Human-AI Leadership Core

The upper-division component advances students into applied AI leadership, strategy, governance, project management, and human-centered interaction design.

  • HAI 300 - Foundations of Human-AI Collaboration
  • HAI 310 - Applied AI for Organizational Decision-Making
  • HAI 320 - Data Analytics and Visualization for Leaders
  • HAI 340 - Organizational Leadership in AI-Enabled Environments
  • HAI 350 - AI Strategy and Implementation
  • HAI 360 - Ethics, Governance, and Responsible AI
  • HAI 370 - Managing AI Projects and Cross-Functional Teams
  • HAI 380 - Human-AI Interaction and Experience Design

Course Development Detail

Proposed upper-division courses translate AI capability into organizational leadership.

The draft course descriptions and learning outcomes below come from the BDP program description work and can support the Program Quality, non-duplication, advisory review, and intersegmental review narratives.

HAI 300 Foundations of Human-AI Collaboration

This course introduces advanced concepts in human-AI collaboration, focusing on how artificial intelligence systems augment human capabilities in organizational settings. Students examine AI capabilities, limitations, and the design of effective human-AI workflows across industries.

  • Analyze the roles of humans and AI systems in collaborative workflows.
  • Evaluate the capabilities and limitations of AI technologies in organizational contexts.
  • Design human-AI collaboration models to improve efficiency and outcomes.
HAI 310 Applied AI for Organizational Decision-Making

This course explores the application of AI tools and technologies to support organizational decision-making. Students learn to interpret AI-generated insights and integrate them into structured decision frameworks.

  • Analyze AI-generated outputs to support business decision-making.
  • Evaluate AI tools for effectiveness in solving organizational problems.
  • Apply decision-making frameworks using AI-driven insights.
HAI 320 Data Analytics and Visualization for Leaders

This course develops advanced data literacy skills for leaders working with AI systems. Students learn to interpret complex datasets, create visualizations, and communicate insights to stakeholders.

  • Interpret complex data outputs and visualizations for decision-making.
  • Develop data visualizations to communicate insights effectively.
  • Evaluate data quality and limitations in AI-driven environments.
HAI 340 Organizational Leadership in AI-Enabled Environments

This course examines leadership strategies for managing organizations undergoing AI-driven transformation, including workforce adaptation, human-AI role design, and leading change in technology-enabled environments.

  • Evaluate leadership strategies for AI-enabled organizations.
  • Design workforce adaptation strategies for AI integration.
  • Apply change management principles to support AI adoption.
HAI 350 AI Strategy and Implementation

This course focuses on the development and execution of AI strategies within organizations. Students assess readiness, identify use cases, and design implementation plans aligned with organizational goals.

  • Develop AI implementation strategies aligned with organizational objectives.
  • Assess organizational readiness for AI adoption.
  • Evaluate the impact and value of AI initiatives.
HAI 360 Ethics, Governance, and Responsible AI

This course explores ethical, legal, and governance issues associated with artificial intelligence. Students examine bias, fairness, accountability, and regulatory considerations in AI systems.

  • Evaluate ethical risks associated with AI systems.
  • Apply governance frameworks for responsible AI implementation.
  • Analyze legal and regulatory considerations related to AI technologies.
HAI 370 Managing AI Projects and Cross-Functional Teams

This course prepares students to manage AI-related projects and collaborate across technical and non-technical teams. Emphasis is placed on project management methodologies, communication, and stakeholder alignment.

  • Apply project management methodologies to AI initiatives.
  • Coordinate communication across cross-functional teams.
  • Evaluate project performance and outcomes.
HAI 380 Human-AI Interaction and Experience Design

This course examines the design of effective human-AI interactions, focusing on usability, trust, and user experience. Students explore how design influences adoption and system performance.

  • Analyze factors influencing user trust and adoption of AI systems.
  • Design human-centered AI interaction frameworks.
  • Evaluate usability and user experience in AI-enabled systems.
HAI 495A AI Implementation Capstone I

Students identify a real-world organizational problem and develop a proposal for an AI-enabled solution. Emphasis is placed on research, feasibility, and project planning.

  • Define a real-world problem appropriate for AI-enabled solutions.
  • Develop a structured project proposal including scope and methodology.
  • Conduct feasibility and stakeholder analysis.
HAI 495B AI Implementation Capstone II

This culminating course requires students to design, implement, and present an AI-enabled solution to an organizational challenge. Students integrate technical, leadership, and ethical considerations.

  • Design and implement an AI-enabled solution addressing an organizational need.
  • Evaluate the effectiveness and impact of the implemented solution.
  • Present findings and recommendations to professional audiences.

KSA Alignment

A workforce competency framework for applied AI leadership.

The National Applied AI Consortium KSA framework is useful as an external benchmark because it separates what students should know, what they should be able to do, and how they should adapt in applied AI workplaces.

Knowledge

AI and data foundations, data governance, analytics, machine learning awareness, generative AI, human-centered design, AI maturity, legal considerations, cloud platforms, systems thinking, NLP, and computer vision awareness.

Skills

Data readiness, visualization, dashboards, prompt engineering, AI tool use, business use-case evaluation, AI-supported problem solving, legal and ethical evaluation, business process automation, and storytelling for business impact.

Abilities

Translate AI into business value, collaborate across technical and business teams, communicate with stakeholders, lead change responsibly, keep current with emerging tools, and apply professional judgment in ambiguous settings.

Capstone

AI Implementation Capstone I & II

Students identify a real organizational problem, develop a proposal for an AI-enabled solution, analyze feasibility and stakeholders, then design, implement, evaluate, and present the solution to a professional audience.
